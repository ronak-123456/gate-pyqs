@@ Q4

concept: Change of base: $\log_{a^{k}} b = \tfrac{1}{k}\log_a b$
level: easy
style: formula
@@
**From the basics.** $\log_a b = \dfrac{\ln b}{\ln a}$. If the base is raised to a power $k$, then $\log_{a^k} b = \dfrac{\ln b}{k\ln a} = \dfrac{1}{k}\log_a b$.

@@ Q14
ans: (C) Tunneling
concept: Zener breakdown = band-to-band quantum tunnelling across a thin, heavily doped junction
level: easy
style: recall
@@
**From the basics.** A reverse-biased pn junction can break down in two ways:
- **Avalanche:** carriers gain enough energy in a wide depletion region to knock out new electron–hole pairs. This happens in lightly doped junctions at high voltage.
- **Zener (tunnelling):** in heavily doped junctions the depletion region is only a few nanometres wide. Under reverse bias, the valence band on the p side lines up with the conduction band on the n side, and electrons *tunnel* straight through the thin barrier.

**Solution.** The mechanism that defines Zener breakdown is **tunnelling**.

**Watch out.** Commercial "Zener diodes" rated above about 6 V actually break down by avalanche. GATE's intended answer, as in 2008 Q55, is the textbook Zener effect: tunnelling.

@@ Q16
ans: (A) Stable and causal response
concept: Pole–zero cancellation in a difference equation
level: medium
style: multi-step
@@
**From the basics.** Taking the z-transform of a difference equation gives $H(z) = Y(z)/X(z)$. A recursion that computes $y[n]$ from past outputs is causal. The response is stable if its remaining poles lie inside $|z| = 1$.

**Solution.**
$$H(z) = \frac{1}{1-\tfrac56 z^{-1}+\tfrac16 z^{-2}} = \frac{1}{(1-\tfrac12 z^{-1})(1-\tfrac13 z^{-1})}.$$
The input is $X(z) = 1 - \tfrac13 z^{-1}$, which cancels one factor:
$$Y(z) = \frac{1}{1-\tfrac12 z^{-1}} \;\Rightarrow\; y[n] = \left(\tfrac12\right)^n u[n].$$
The response is right-sided (causal) and decays, since its pole is at $0.5$, inside the unit circle. So it is **stable and causal**.

**From the basics.** In static CMOS the output is pulled **up** through PMOS transistors, which conduct when their gate is 0, and pulled **down** through NMOS transistors, which conduct when their gate is 1. The two networks are duals: series in one means parallel in the other.

@@ Q24
